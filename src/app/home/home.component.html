About

Houman Homayoun is a Professor of Electrical and Computer Engineering at UC Davis, leading the NSF Center for Hardware and Embedded Systems Security and Trust.

Under his direction, CHEST tackles some of the most pressing security challenges facing the world today — helping to ensure the safety and reliability of hardware and embedded systems.

History

Prior to his current position, Houman held an Associate Professorship in Electrical and Computer Engineering at George Mason University. He was a recipient of the Computing Innovation (CI) Fellowship from the CRA-CCC, during which he spent two years at UC San Diego. He completed his Ph.D. in Computer Science at UC Irvine, where he was awarded the four-year Computer Science Department Chair Fellowship.

He earned his Ph.D. from UC Irvine in 2010, an MS in computer engineering from the University of Victoria in 2005, and a BS in electrical engineering from Sharif University of Technology in 2003.

His research spans hardware security and trust, applied machine learning and AI, data-intensive computing, and heterogeneous computing. He has published more than 200 technical papers and directed over $10M in research funding from NSF, DARPA, AFRL, NIST, the US Congress, and industry sponsors — earning best-paper recognition at GLSVLSI, ICCAD, ICDM, DCAS, ISVLSI, and DATE.

CHEST has received congressional support for hardware-security research, included in the 2021 National Defense Authorization Act. He has served on Virginia's Cybersecurity Research and Technology Commercialization working group and as an Associate Editor of IEEE Transactions on VLSI since 2017.
